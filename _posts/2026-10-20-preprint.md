I’m tremendously proud/relieved to say that our work making geospatial models of Japanese encephalitis virus (JEV) risk in Australia is finally a <a href="https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.15.26365607">preprint</a>! 

This project started back in 2022, following the sudden outbreak of JEV on the Australian mainland. In a single summer, JEV appeared to spread from northern Australia over 3,000 km to central Victoria, my home state.

JEV is spread by Culex mosquitoes to domestic and feral pigs, and wading birds, including herons and egrets. People are dead-end hosts. To map relative risk of JEV, we took the approach of separately modelling habitat suitability (the potential ecological niche, where minimum conditions exist for virus transmission) and transmission intensity (the realised ecological niche, where the virus was observed during the outbreak, accounting for surveillance effort). Our model of JEV habitat suitability incorporated separate models of wading bird habitat and Culex mosquito habitat, already published in Duncan et al., 2026 (<https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pntd.0014127>). Putting these models together, we were able to limit our transmission intensity estimate based on conditions expected to support transmission. We were also able to estimate the potential extent of transmission given the environmental conditions at the time.

The project required putting together data of JEV surveillance and detection from 17 Government departments spanning health, agriculture, and biosecurity. Many, many hours went into producing these data at a jurisdictional level and we put many further hours into working with data custodians to harmonise the data together to represent nation-wide surveillance efforts.

Our research was supported by the Commonwealth Department of Health and Aged Care and delivered at the time of the outbreak through two reports to Government. It was used to support the spatial prioritisation of response efforts in several jurisdictions.

Congrats go to our analysis team, David Duncan, Nick Golding, and Freya Shearer; my wonderful PhD supervisors Jennifer Flegg, David Price, and Freya; our co-authors who assisted in getting this manuscript off the ground; and all of our other colleagues who provided their expertise and support over the years we’ve been putting this project together.
